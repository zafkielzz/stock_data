### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2022, Novaland Group’s (HOSE: NVL) operational performance was severely impacted by macroeconomic headwinds and unprecedented distress in the domestic real estate market. Consolidated total revenue reached nearly VND 11,151 billion, contracting by 25.5% year-over-year (YoY) and achieving just over 30% of the annual target. Concurrently, consolidated net profit after tax stood at nearly VND 2,182 billion, representing a 36.8% YoY decline and fulfilling merely 33% of the full-year guidance. This underperformance was primarily driven by escalating inflation, credit tightening policies, bottlenecks in capital-raising channels, rising interest rate environments, and protracted project legal bottlenecks. In this challenging operating climate, the enterprise's core growth drivers pivoted toward comprehensive balance-sheet restructuring, maximizing focus on its core business of real estate investment and development (including flagship projects such as Aqua City, NovaWorld Phan Thiet, NovaWorld Ho Tram, and developments in central HCMC), while actively negotiating to resolve legal impediments and secure liquidity injections to maintain ongoing operations.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
Novaland's capital structure and cash flow profile faced acute pressures in 2022 stemming from funding and credit obligations. The Group maintained short- and long-term borrowings with floating interest rates, wherein unsecured short-term loans carried interest rates ranging from 4.0% to 18.0% p.a., while long-term borrowings accrued interest between 13.0% and 13.2% p.a. Borrowing costs remained a substantial financial burden, with total capitalized borrowing costs capitalized into inventory reaching VND 6,106 billion for the year, utilizing capitalization rates ranging from 3.7% to 15.5% p.a. To secure debt service and repayment obligations for corporate bonds and bank credit facilities, the Group and its subsidiaries pledged a significant volume of assets as collateral, including land use rights in Thanh My Loi Ward (Thu Duc City, HCMC). Furthermore, the scale of inventories pledged as collateral for borrowings surged significantly to VND 57,675 billion, exerting substantial strain on short-term liquidity management.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Novaland's risk management and provisioning framework reflected a conservative stance against overdue receivables and inventory exposures. As of year-end 2022, the balance of overdue short-term trade receivables stood at VND 10,635,645,778, while overdue long-term other receivables were recorded at VND 105,429,508. Regarding inventory, properties under development accounted for the majority of the portfolio, primarily comprising land use fees, architectural design consulting, and construction costs for ongoing projects, alongside completed properties ready for sale at developments such as Lucky Dragon (District 9), Orchard Garden (Phu Nhuan District), and Tropic Garden 2 (Thu Duc City). Protracted legal bottlenecks compounded by a constricted debt-capital market introduced contingent cash flow risks, necessitating active engagement by the Executive Board with financial partners and municipal authorities to resolve post-balance-sheet contingencies.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
As part of its sustainable development strategy, Novaland continued to embed international standards into its operations via its Integrated Report, aligning with the International Integrated Reporting Council (IIRC) framework, the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards for Sustainability Reporting, and OECD Corporate Governance Principles. On the Environmental (E) front, the Group prioritized environmental impact management, promoted sustainable procurement practices, and collaborated with organizations such as Packaging Recovery Organization Vietnam (PRO Vietnam) to launch "Green Living Day" campaigns aimed at enhancing awareness of waste sorting and recyclable collection at the source. On the Social (S) front, Novaland focused on human capital investment and development through educational and training programs, ensuring customer satisfaction, health, and safety, while upholding corporate social responsibility via charitable funds and social security initiatives. On the Governance (G) front, the enterprise committed to strict compliance with stock market disclosure regulations, ensuring transparency under the independent audit of PwC (Vietnam) Ltd. while aligning its operational model with international best practices.
