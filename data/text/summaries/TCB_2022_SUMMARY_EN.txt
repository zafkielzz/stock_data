### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In fiscal year 2022, Vietnam Technological and Commercial Joint Stock Bank (TCB) navigated a highly challenging and volatile market backdrop, reflected in a -48.80% drawdown in its share price (declining from an opening price of VND 23,360 to a closing price of VND 11,960, trading within a 52-week range of VND 8,930 to VND 25,400, with an average matched trading volume of 6,669,486 shares per session). To adapt, the Bank's overarching strategy centered on building operational capabilities for financial and non-financial risk management, integrating these competencies across three strategic pillars: digitization, data, and talent. The Bank proactively upgraded its risk governance framework, focusing on robust infrastructure and operating models to support business transformation, maintain market leadership, and manage emerging risks such as model risk, cyber security risk, and contagion risk. The deployment of sophisticated data models enhanced capabilities in detecting, preventing, and mitigating cyber-attacks as well as containing contagion risk, thereby aligning risk management standards more closely with international benchmarks, including Basel III compliance and State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) regulations.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
To address rising interest rate cycles and liquidity tightening, TCB implemented advanced measurement approaches for Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB) by adopting a yield curve methodology for the banking book, facilitating precise valuation of the market value and fair value of individual assets and liabilities. The Bank measures, backtests, and monitors the Delta Economic Value of Equity (Delta EVE) metric under Basel III standards, combining this with Current Account Savings Account (CASA) behavioral modeling to accurately reflect its interest rate risk profile. Furthermore, TCB established a benchmark methodology framework to measure and monitor basis risk, while successfully deploying an Asset-Liability Management (ALM) platform provided by a world-leading software and data analytics firm. This infrastructure supports the execution of liquidity risk management strategies, incorporating stochastic modeling and stress testing under various macroeconomic and funding volatility scenarios.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
In credit risk management and provisioning, TCB designed and digitized a new credit operating model, leveraging the Corporate Credit Decision Engine (BCDE) infrastructure deployed as part of its steadfast commitment to continuous improvement. The Bank re-engineered core processes and upskilled personnel to better serve corporate clients throughout the credit risk lifecycle. Implemented initiatives include the refinement of credit scoring models, workflow automation of documentation, risk-based profiling to optimize document checklist requirements, underwriting resource allocation, real-time decision support for participants in the corporate credit value chain, the end-to-end digitization of underwriting and early warning processes, and the implementation of a new post-lending control workflow on the BCDE system. Across key business hubs, processing turnaround times dropped by approximately 30% for credit approval and volume metrics, yielding roughly a 20-fold improvement in customer experience. Concurrently, TCB pursued its strategic objective of loan portfolio diversification through the nationwide rollout of the "Smart Credit" retail loan origination platform for all unsecured and secured lending products, pairing a redesigned customer journey with optimized credit models to elevate client experience.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
- **Environmental (E):** The Bank focused on upgrading its governance infrastructure and operating models to support sustainable business transformation, coupled with the application of advanced technologies to optimize resource efficiency and monitor environmental impacts across its operational value chain.
- **Social (S):** TCB continued to attract top-tier analytical talent, investing heavily in human capital development via specialized training programs to empower its workforce to better serve clients, while fostering a professional, transparent work environment and ensuring comprehensive employee welfare.
- **Governance (G):** The Bank remained steadfast in adhering to the highest standards of transparency and corporate governance in alignment with the directives of the SBV and stakeholders; enhanced compliance capabilities regarding international standards such as Basel III; and reinforced institutional independence and stringent controls over risk management across all three strategic pillars to ensure safe, efficient, and sustainable banking operations.
