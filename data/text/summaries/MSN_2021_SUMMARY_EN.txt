### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
Fiscal year 2021 marked a highly robust trading period for MSN shares, delivering an impressive price appreciation of +89.68% as the closing price advanced to VND 141,520 from an opening price of VND 74,610 (trading within a 52-week range of VND 65,590 to VND 144,830, with an average daily trading volume of 1,883,039 shares). Masan Group’s core growth catalyst stems from its "Point of Life" (POL) strategy, anchored by the successful piloting of the integrated O2 (online-to-offline) mini-mall retail model. This model effectively resolves the retail unit economics challenge in rural demographics—where the vast majority of consumers reside—by lowering the break-even revenue per store per day from over VND 20 million to approximately VND 14 million. The mini-mall ecosystem consolidates essential consumer goods (WinMart), financial services (Techcombank), tea and coffee (Phúc Long), and mobile telecommunication services (Reddi), thereby increasing share-of-wallet capture for essential spending from 25% to 60%. Furthermore, the deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) algorithms to optimize product portfolio curation, refine store site selection (elevating the site success rate to 90%), and streamline Know Your Customer (KYC) protocols has empowered the Group to hyper-personalize customer experiences, drive operating leverage, and expand net margins across the entire value chain.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
In managing its capital structure and financial obligations, the Group extensively utilized promissory notes and vanilla bonds to finance its scaling operations. Per the financial footnote disclosures, bonds issued upon initial recognition are measured at historical cost (comprising gross proceeds net of transaction costs), with discounts, premiums, and issuance expenses amortized on a straight-line basis over the tenor of the instruments. For long-term obligations such as employee retirement benefits, present values are discounted using yields on high-quality corporate bonds rated AA with equivalent maturities. Additionally, borrowing costs and capital capitalization structures were impacted by business combination transactions and long-term capital allocation strategies directed toward closed-loop supply chains. Nevertheless, cash flow pressures were materially alleviated by the retail mini-mall model's optimized break-even thresholds and the strategic partnership with De Heus (involving the divestiture and transfer of Masan MEATLife’s feed segment), which collectively fortified the balance sheet and redirected capital resources toward core branded meat and consumer-packaged goods.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
The Group’s risk management and accounting estimation frameworks adhere strictly to prudent recognition principles. Long-term asset items, such as mining rights and deferred stripping costs incurred during mine development and production phases, are amortized systematically based on estimated mineral reserves. During the year, Nui Phao Mining (an indirect subsidiary) updated its mineral reserve and resource estimates for the Nui Phao Project following independent expert technical assessments, resulting in a VND 8,799 million reduction in amortization expense for the period. Regarding mineral revenue recognition, selling prices are typically provisionally priced and subsequently finalized within 30 to 180 days post-dispatch based on market fluctuations, necessitating stringent provisioning mechanisms and fair-value estimations for trade receivables. Accruals for employee benefits, tax liabilities, and long-term financial obligations are subjected to periodic actuarial and statistical valuation. Notably, the comprehensive strategic alliance executed with De Heus in November 2021 served as a pivotal financial inflection point, structurally re-engineering the agricultural segment and fully transforming Masan MEATLife into a pure-play branded meat enterprise, thereby substantially mitigating commodity input price volatility risks across the 3F (Feed-Farm-Food) value chain.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
Masan Group steadfastly pursues its sustainable development philosophy of *"Doing well by doing good"* to elevate the material and spiritual well-being of nearly 100 million Vietnamese citizens, executing this commitment synchronously across four core pillars:
- **Environmental (E):** The Group prioritizes responsible supply chain management, highlighted by collaborative initiatives with fishermen and research institutions (such as the Research Institute for Marine Fisheries) to safeguard anchovy stocks and mitigate overfishing, thereby preserving ecosystem equilibrium. Raw material and packaging suppliers are mandated to strictly comply with biosafety standards, non-GMO protocols, and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), alongside optimizing energy efficiency and material circularity across processing facilities.
- **Social (S):** The Group focuses on sustainable human capital development, securing stable employment for tens of thousands of direct and indirect workers throughout the value chain—spanning fish sauce barrel houses, livestock farms, and the modern retail network. Within the agricultural segment, Masan MEATLife transfers biosafety farming technologies—entirely free of growth hormones and antibiotics—to partner farms, guaranteeing a supply of clean, safe, and reasonably priced food products for consumers.
- **Governance (G):** Corporate governance practices adhere to the highest standards of transparency, placing the consumer at the heart of every strategic resolution passed by the Board of Directors. Financial and sustainability disclosures are subjected to independent external audits and remain in strict compliance with prevailing regulatory frameworks, thereby safeguarding the legitimate rights and interests of shareholders and all stakeholders.
