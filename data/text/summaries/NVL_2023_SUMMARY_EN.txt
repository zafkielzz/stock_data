### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2023, Novaland Group’s financial performance clearly reflected the challenges of the broader macroeconomic environment alongside its comprehensive corporate restructuring. For the 12-month cumulative period, the Group recorded consolidated net revenue of VND 4,757 billion and net profit after tax of VND 486 billion. Net revenue from product sales accounted for nearly VND 4,090 billion, primarily driven by the handover of units across key developments, including NovaWorld Phan Thiet, NovaWorld Ho Tram, Aqua City, Palm City, Lakeview City, Saigon Royal, and other downtown real estate projects. Additionally, net revenue from service provision contributed VND 667 billion. 

Actual top-line performance fell short of the targets presented to the Annual General Meeting of Shareholders in June 2023 due to a contraction in real estate transfer revenues, stemming from dampened market sentiment and an extended recovery horizon. Conversely, net profit after tax exceeded guidance, underpinned by debt restructuring efforts, operational expenditure optimization, and financial income realized during the restructuring phase. Regarding equity market performance, the ticker NVL opened the year at VND 13,050 and closed at VND 15,890, translating to a full-year return of +21.76% (within a 52-week trading range of VND 9,370 to VND 20,740), alongside an average matched trading volume of 27,836,820 shares per session.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
Novaland’s financial restructuring in 2023 demonstrated notable progress in addressing its debt obligations. As of year-end, the Group successfully pared down its debt load by approximately VND 7,156 billion compared to 2022. Key debt-restructuring milestones included finalizing the restructuring terms for a USD 300 million convertible bond issuance, achieving substantial success in extending the maturity of domestic corporate bonds under Decree 08/2023/NĐ-CP, and executing a bond-for-equity swap valued at VND 2,346 billion in a project-level subsidiary, alongside other bilateral swap agreements. 

Nevertheless, liquidity pressures and capital structure constraints persist, with total assets contracting by 6.3% year-to-date to VND 241,486 billion as of December 31, 2023. Inventory remained exceptionally elevated at VND 138,935 billion, of which land bank and properties under development constituted the vast majority at VND 129,574 billion (equivalent to 93.3%). Total capitalized borrowing costs for the year reached VND 6,368 billion, carrying effective interest rates ranging from 3.5% p.a. to 16.5% p.a. Cash, cash equivalents, and term deposits yielded interest rates between 1.9% p.a. and 9.5% p.a. depending on the tenor, with a portion pledged as collateral for borrowings, utilized for guarantees, or subjected to escrow controls by lending institutions per project-specific usage covenants.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Asset portfolio and accounts receivable risk management warranted rigorous oversight due to certain overdue credit exposures. Specifically, overdue short-term trade receivables stood at VND 10,635,645,778, while overdue other short-term receivables amounted to VND 105,429,508 (associated with entities such as Hoang Phuc Housing Construction and Investment Co., Ltd., Thinh Vuong Decor Co., Ltd., Saigon Industrial Services and Electronics JSC, and Hung Hiep Phat Investment Trading JSC). 

Regarding post-balance sheet events, the Notes to the Financial Statements recorded that receivables linked to Saigon Gon under the Capital Transfer Agreement Cancellation Agreement (comprising VND 645,444,132,075, alongside the refund of VND 388,560,000,000 in principal capital contributions and associated late-payment interest) were recovered in full prior to the issuance of the consolidated financial statements. Furthermore, collateral pledged against debt facilities comprised inventories valued at VND 57,199 billion, cash and cash equivalents of VND 63 billion, and short-term bank deposits of VND 14 billion. Protracted legal bottlenecks across several developments remain a latent risk factor impacting delivery schedules and customer commitments, necessitating Novaland’s ongoing collaboration with regulatory authorities to resolve these hurdles.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
Novaland integrated its Sustainability Report into its 2023 Integrated Annual Report, adhering to reputable international standards and best practices.
- **Environmental (E):** The Group prioritizes the development of master-planned products oriented toward open-space green designs, sustainability, and low carbon emissions, while researching environmentally friendly solutions to match evolving customer demands and enhance long-term real estate value.
- **Social (S):** Human capital strategy during the year focused on elevating operational quality and efficiency. The company emphasized talent management, workforce education and training, and the safeguarding of employee welfare and occupational safety. Concurrently, urban township development initiatives continued to be advanced hand-in-hand with social welfare programs, local economic development, and the promotion of national tourism.
- **Governance (G):** The corporate governance framework operates under a tiered model comprising the General Meeting of Shareholders, the Board of Directors, the Chief Executive Officer, and the Board-level Audit Committee, working in tandem with specialized committees including the ESG-Risk Management-Quality Control Committee and the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. The FY2023 financial statements were audited by PwC (Vietnam) Ltd., ensuring consistency, transparency, and full compliance with the disclosure mandates of Circular 96/2020/TT-BTC, aligned with GRI Standards, the International Integrated Reporting Council (IIRC) framework, and OECD Corporate Governance Principles.
