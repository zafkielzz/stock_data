Below is the institutional-grade equity research translation of the financial summary for Saigon - Hanoi Commercial Joint Stock Bank (SHB):

### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In fiscal year 2022, SHB’s business operations took place against a backdrop of complex volatility in the financial and monetary markets, accompanied by severe pressure from systemic liquidity and an escalating interest rate environment. The trading performance of SHB shares clearly reflected the broader downward pressure of the stock market, with the share price opening the year at VND 12,030 and closing at VND 6,120, representing a 49.13% decline for the full year (trading within a 52-week low of VND 4,410 and a high of VND 12,940, with average matched trading volume reaching 11,804,616 shares/session). The bank’s core growth drivers during the year stemmed from maintaining a prudent credit scale, accelerating comprehensive digital transformation to optimize operating expenses, and diversifying retail financial service products. However, due to its profile as a domestic financial service provider, direct export business segments and markets are not applicable; growth instead relied primarily on capturing domestic credit market opportunities, payment services, insurance fee income, and trade financing for the corporate community.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, BORROWING & INTEREST RATES
Reflecting the operational characteristics of a commercial bank, SHB’s capital structure is primarily composed of customer deposits, borrowings from the Government and the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV), deposits and borrowings from other credit institutions, alongside shareholders' equity derived from charter capital and reserve funds. Pressure on the cost of funds (COF) increased significantly in 2022 due to the global monetary tightening trend and fierce competition for deposit interest rates among joint-stock commercial banks to ensure liquidity ratios and capital safety limits mandated by the SBV. The structure of the credit and funding portfolios was directly influenced by interbank market rate fluctuations and floating lending rates tied to deposit rate term structures. Capital structure risk was concentrated primarily in maturity mismatch pressures between assets (medium- and long-term loans) and liabilities (short-term funding), requiring the bank to maintain flexible liquidity reserve ratios and exercise stringent management over interest rate risk limits.

### 3. PROVISIONING RISK, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & SUBSEQUENT EVENTS
SHB’s credit risk provisioning was executed in strict compliance with current regulations set by the SBV (notably Circular 11/2021/TT-NHNN) to accurately reflect asset quality and adequately cover risk-latent debts amid the financial health deterioration of a segment of customers. Off-balance-sheet commitments and guarantees (letters of credit [L/Cs], loan guarantees, and payment guarantees) continued to play a vital role in supporting corporate trade finance while concurrently harboring potential credit conversion risks. Regarding events arising after December 31, 2022, the Bank successfully completed the disclosure of its 2022 Annual Report, publishing it on the bank’s electronic portal on April 12, 2023, under the direction of Chairman of the Board of Directors Do Quang Hien, thereby ensuring compliance with current statutory disclosure transparency for entities listed on the Ho Chi Minh City Stock Exchange (HoSE).

### 4. ESG EXECUTION
- **Environment (E):** SHB has progressively integrated environmental criteria and climate change risk management into its credit portfolio, restricting capital allocation to projects with high environmental pollution risks. The bank has promoted a digital transformation strategy to reduce paper consumption and operational energy usage across branches and transaction offices system-wide, while channeling green credit flows into sustainable development and renewable energy projects.
- **Social (S):** SHB maintains a professional working environment, ensuring employee welfare, occupational safety, gender equality, and fair advancement opportunities for all staff members. Training programs aimed at enhancing professional competencies and risk management expertise are conducted regularly. Additionally, the bank actively participates in nationwide social welfare, gratitude, and community support programs.
- **Governance (G):** SHB operates in compliance with a transparent public corporate governance model, featuring relative independence and rigorous oversight from the Board of Directors, the Supervisory Board, and specialized sub-committees. Financial reporting and information disclosure are independently audited by top-tier auditing firms, ensuring a high degree of compliance with market transparency standards and sustainable development orientations.
