### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In fiscal year 2025, Saigon Thuong Tin Commercial Joint Stock Bank (Sacombank - STB) registered positive balance sheet expansion, though profitability experienced downward pressure due to a conservative provisioning strategy. Specifically, consolidated profit before tax reached only 52% of the annual target as the bank proactively frontloaded credit risk provisions to fortify its loss-absorbing buffers and enhance asset quality. Regarding core drivers, the Retail Banking segment continued to serve as the bedrock for sustainable retail growth; the Corporate Banking division focused on operational model innovation, partnership expansion, and capability enhancement; the Digital Banking division spearheaded the digital transformation roadmap by deploying the Retail LOS system, digitizing international trade finance workflows, and rolling out over-the-counter Tablet devices; while the Financial Markets division cemented its role as a vital revenue-generating engine. Additionally, STB accelerated the recovery and resolution of over VND 17 trillion in non-performing loans (NPLs) and legacy assets, generating upwards of VND 10.7 trillion in recoveries to fuel provisioning requirements.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
STB’s capital structure and balance sheet were decisively reinforced in 2025, with total assets scaling to VND 917,120 billion—representing a 22.6% year-to-date increase and achieving 112% of the full-year target. Total mobilized funds reached VND 836,379 billion, up 23.9%, underpinned by a robust 114.4% surge in Tier-2 funding (interbank and wholesale funding). This strategy successfully diversified the funding mix, mitigated reliance on retail customer deposits, and enhanced asset-liability management (ALM) flexibility amid interest rate volatility. Equity stood at VND 59,867 billion (up 8.9%), comprising charter capital of VND 18,852 billion and cumulative retained earnings of VND 31,294 billion (amounting to 1.7x charter capital). Liquidity and solvency ratios continued to hover comfortably above regulatory minima, preserving robust capital cushions and safeguarding against interest rate and liquidity risks.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Risk management and provisioning took precedence for STB throughout fiscal year 2025. Executive management proactively ramped up credit risk provisioning charges to decisively remediate legacy assets and NPLs, reflecting a prudent management philosophy aimed at bolstering defensive capabilities against escalating macroeconomic headwinds. Within the framework of the Restructuring Project, the bank successfully resolved the substantial bulk of its NPL portfolio, fulfilled pending financial obligations, and largely rectified historical cross-ownership and equity investment overhangs. The operational network was proactively streamlined in alignment with new administrative boundaries, involving the relocation of 80 transaction points and a headcount reduction of 1,272 personnel to optimize labor productivity and ensure seamless, stable post-restructuring operations system-wide.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
- **Environmental (E):** STB vigorously drove comprehensive paperless digitalization while channeling green credit allocations toward sustainable production, agriculture, forestry, fishery, and import-export sectors, fully aligned with the strategic directives of the Government and the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) to foster sustainable development.
- **Social (S):** The bank prioritized human capital development, maintaining a consolidated workforce of 16,816 employees by year-end 2025, implemented concurrently with organizational streamlining to elevate operational efficiency. Comprehensive human resource policies, professional code of ethics, and impactful corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiatives were systematically executed to deliver tangible value to the community.
- **Governance (G):** STB strictly adhered to corporate governance benchmarks, enhanced information transparency in accordance with international best practices, and strengthened supervisory effectiveness. Brand equity was further validated as Moody’s Ratings upgraded several key credit metrics (local and foreign currency deposit ratings and issuer ratings to B1, alongside a Baseline Credit Assessment (BCA) upgrade to b2) with a Stable outlook, laying a solid foundation for expanding domestic and cross-border institutional partnerships.
