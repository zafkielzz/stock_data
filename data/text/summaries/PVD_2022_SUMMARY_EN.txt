### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
PVD’s operational and financial performance in FY2022 experienced a positive recovery, underpinned by favorable macro dynamics in regional and global petroleum markets. Core earnings growth was principally driven by the drilling division, where the commercial deployment of the company-owned rig fleet was executed with high efficiency. The entire owned rig fleet maintained continuous and stable utilization commencing in Q2 2022, resulting in year-on-year (YoY) improvements in both operating efficiency and average daily rates (ADRs). Specifically, PVD operated an average of 3.4 jack-up rigs (vs. 3.2 rigs in FY2021) alongside 0.6 leased rigs (vs. 0.3 rigs in FY2021). Average jack-up ADRs rose by 17% YoY, complemented by stellar operational uptime exceeding 99% across the fleet (PV DRILLING I achieved 99.52%, PV DRILLING II 99.9%, PV DRILLING III 99.87%, PV DRILLING V 98.17%, PV DRILLING VI 99.82%, and the land rig PV DRILLING 11 reached 98.44%). 

Internationally, PVD sustained its footprint in Malaysia—deploying the PV DRILLING III for Hibiscus—and Algeria, where the PV DRILLING 11 operated continuously for Groupement Bir Seba. Notably, PVD successfully penetrated the technically stringent and protectionist Indonesian market by securing its maiden drilling contract for PHE ONWJ in West Java, which was swiftly followed by a follow-on contract award spanning up to 3 years. In Brunei, the deepwater semi-submersible rig PV DRILLING V completed its inaugural year serving Brunei Shell Petroleum (BSP) by delivering 5 completed wells—beating the schedule by 2 wells—while setting multiple technical drilling records.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
PVD’s capital structure remains subject to considerable leverage pressures stemming from long-term borrowings utilized to finance the construction of ultra-specification rigs such as PV DRILLING V and PV DRILLING VI. The majority of these facilities carry floating interest rates indexed to LIBOR (3-month or 6-month tenor plus a fixed credit spread). Key lending institutions providing credit lines to the Corporation include Vietcombank, MBBank, Vietinbank, OceanBank, SeABank, and TPBank. Structural solvency risks and liquidity strains are acutely manifested at the subsidiary level, evidenced by defaults on principal amortizations, interest expenses, and default penalty charges. Specifically, PVD Deepwater recorded mature long-term debt obligations financing the PV DRILLING V rig amounting to VND 353,940,930,161 (equivalent to USD 14,915,336), alongside outstanding accrued interest of VND 68,800,278,295 and overdue penalty interest of VND 34,392,476,935. Similarly, PVD Overseas reported mature long-term debt maturities for the PV DRILLING VI rig totaling VND 317,088,450,000 (equivalent to USD 13,545,000, of which VND 103,004,000,000 is past due at Vietcombank), accompanied by accrued interest of VND 123,841,610,596 and penalty charges of VND 21,011,085,834. PVD Deepwater has formally petitioned for debt service deferrals, receiving constructive acknowledgments from MBBank, Vietinbank, and OceanBank, whereas PVD Overseas has yet to receive formal notifications from Vietcombank regarding the overdue status.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
PVD maintains a prudent provisioning policy regarding asset quality and credit risks. Concerning the doubtful trade receivables due from KrisEnergy (Apsara) Company Limited pertaining to the Apsara field drilling campaign in Cambodia—whose parent entity, KrisEnergy Limited, filed for liquidation in the Cayman Islands in 2021—Management has provisioned VND 75,861,252,090 against total outstanding receivables as of December 31, 2022, while actively collaborating with diplomatic and regulatory authorities to pursue recovery. Additionally, PVD recorded an incremental inventory obsolescence provision of VND 11,657,688,560 based on asset condition assessments and future economic utility. Regarding post-balance sheet tax liabilities, PVD Tech recognized corporate income tax (CIT) obligations associated with the leasing of the Drilling Equipment Set (DES) in Brunei amounting to VND 7,593,017,627; this liability has been settled locally in Brunei and claimed for tax credits under the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement between Vietnam and Brunei.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
- **Environmental (E):** PVD is committed to aligning its operational footprint with stringent Health, Safety, Environment, and Quality (HSEQ) standards. The Corporation strictly adheres to international frameworks such as the ISO 14001:2015 environmental management system, leveraging technical innovations and engineering upgrades to optimize energy intensity and mitigate ecological disruption across onshore and offshore drilling operations.
- **Social (S):** Occupational health and safety metrics reached exemplary benchmarks, highlighted by sustained Zero LTI (Lost Time Injury) milestones certified by the International Association of Drilling Contractors (IADC) across multiple rigs (e.g., PV DRILLING I attained 15 years, PV DRILLING II 13 years, and PV DRILLING V 10 years). PVD prioritizes human capital development, enforces stringent local content compliance in overseas markets, and robustly safeguards employee welfare rights.
- **Governance (G):** PVD operates an advanced corporate governance architecture underpinned by enterprise IT infrastructure (Oracle ERP), international certifications including ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 45001:2018, the Maximo computerized maintenance management system, alongside COSO and ISO 31000 risk management frameworks. Transparent information disclosure and rigorous adherence to best corporate governance practices earned PVD recognition in the "Top 5 Best Corporate Governance Enterprises" and accolades throughout its 15-year participation in the Listed Companies Awards.
