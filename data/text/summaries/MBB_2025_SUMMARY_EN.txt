1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2025, Military Commercial Joint Stock Bank (MBB) recorded robust growth, capitalizing on the macroeconomic recovery (with GDP growth printing at 8.02%). Total assets surpassed VND 1.6 trillion (Note: typically quoted in quadrillions or millions of billions in VN, translated strictly as VND 1.6 quadrillion / VND 1,600 trillion), expanding 43% year-to-date—marking the fastest pace among private-sector peers. Core growth momentum was anchored by gross credit expansion exceeding VND 1 trillion (VND 1 quadrillion), surging 37% to accommodate capital demands from the broader economy, corporates, and key strategic projects. Furthermore, the Current Account Savings Account (CASA) ratio was sustained at approximately 36%, placing the bank at the top tier of the system and optimizing its cost of funds (COF) backed by a 35-million-strong customer base (a 13-fold increase over five years). The cost-to-income ratio (CIR) was prudently managed at ~29%, coming in below the industry average. The financial conglomerate model continued to unlock synergistic value as earnings contributions from 8 subsidiaries (including MBS, MBCapital, MIC, MB Life, and Mcredit) edged up to 8.9%, driving consolidated profit before tax (PBT) to VND 34,268 billion (up nearly 19% YoY).

2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, CREDIT & INTEREST RATES
MB's capital structure was substantially bolstered through the diversification of funding channels, spanning customer deposits, issuance of valuable papers, and medium- to long-term borrowings. Throughout FY2025, the Board of Directors (BoD) approved multiple bond issuance plans (comprising Tier 2 subordinated debt and privately placed vanilla bonds) to secure medium- and long-term funding for credit deployment and ensure capital adequacy ratios remain compliant. The bank effectively maintained its Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) and Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) in strict alignment with Basel III standards. Pressures on the cost of funds (COF) were significantly mitigated by retaining a high CASA weighting across both retail and corporate segments. Additionally, proactive repurchases of certain bond tranches ahead of maturity upon issuers' requests underscored the bank's ample liquidity buffer and proactive financial cost management.

3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
MB’s asset quality remained stringently controlled in FY2025 amidst rapid credit scaling. The non-performing loan (NPL) ratio was contained below 1.3%, successfully meeting projected targets and cementing a low-NPL trajectory for the medium term. Debt management and recovery capabilities were elevated via the rollout of Project DC 5.0, embedding data analytics and automated frameworks across the entire lifecycle of delinquent debt management (from early- to late-stage arrears) to optimize recovery yields. Regarding post-balance sheet events, the BoD executed legal procedures regarding operational network rationalization (such as approving the closure of the Representative Office in Russia pending regulatory filings) alongside related-party transactions, adhering strictly to statutory regulations and market-standard information transparency.

4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
- **Environmental (E):** MB formulated and issued its 2025 Green Credit Framework, aligned with international standards and the Paris Agreement, which received independent assurance from KPMG India confirming compliance with 4 core components. Green credit outstanding reached over VND 68 trillion as of December 2025, of which 80% was channeled into renewable and clean energy, 11% into green agriculture and forestry, and 2% into sustainable water management. 100% of credit proposals underwent environmental risk screening pursuant to Circular 17/2022/TT-NHNN, mandating clients to commit to environmental and social action plans for high-risk projects.
- **Social (S):** The bank focuses on cultivating high-caliber human capital, refining its remuneration framework, and fostering a professional working environment. Serving a customer base of 35 million, MB prioritizes driving financial inclusion, safeguarding consumer rights, and executing corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiatives to generate sustainable value for the broader community.
- **Governance (G):** MB remains a frontrunner in adopting advanced international governance standards. In FY2025, the bank completed the validation and implementation of Basel III in partnership with independent auditor PwC, focusing on core metrics including CAR, LCR, and NSFR. Concurrently, sustainability reporting was benchmarked against the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards (Universal Standards 2021) and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), ensuring transparency and rigorous compliance with securities regulations and public-company governance best practices.
