Based on the corporate disclosure by Saigon Beer - Alcohol - Beverage Corporation (SABECO) dated April 19, 2022, regarding the release of its 2021 Annual Report, alongside verified market data, below is a standard qualitative analysis summary for institutional investors:

### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
Fiscal year 2021 proved to be a challenging period for SABECO due to severe disruptions caused by prolonged social distancing measures and widespread supply chain bottlenecks resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly in key southern markets. The core brewing and distribution business faced immense pressure from gathering restrictions and the closure of on-premise consumption channels. However, growth momentum was sustained through a strategic focus on optimizing operating expenses, scaling up modern trade channels, and leveraging flagship brand equity to defend market share. The domestic market remained the backbone of operations, while exports served as a crucial supplementary channel to partially cushion the decline in domestic purchasing power. SAB’s stock price performance during the year clearly reflected market caution amid macroeconomic uncertainties, opening at VND 72,000 and closing the year at VND 55,210 (a decline of 23.32%). The stock traded within a 52-week range of VND 51,120 to VND 76,540, with an average daily trading volume of 166,797 shares.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, INDEBTEDNESS & INTEREST RATES
SABECO’s capital structure maintained a high degree of safety in 2021, characterized by a low financial leverage ratio and strong capital self-sufficiency underpinned by a robust cash reserve accumulated over years of operations. The company minimized its reliance on external bank borrowings, effectively insulating itself against floating interest rate volatility amid turbulent global financial markets. Short-term and long-term debt (where applicable) were prudently managed to fund short-term working capital at optimized costs through major, reputable commercial banks in Vietnam. Cash flow pressures primarily stemmed from delayed receivables collection and lengthened inventory turnover cycles during the social distancing period; however, overall liquidity risk remained tightly controlled at a very safe level, supported by a healthy balance sheet and minimal principal and interest repayment obligations.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE-SHEET EVENTS
Provisioning in 2021 was executed conservatively by SABECO to accurately reflect potential losses from inventory devaluation and uncollectible receivables arising from business partners facing financial distress. Off-balance-sheet commitments and contingent liabilities were strictly monitored through internal control systems. Regarding legal and litigation matters, no material lawsuits were recorded that would pose a direct threat to the company’s financial standing. Financial events occurring after December 31, 2021, were largely aligned with the broader economic recovery trend as "new normal" operations resumed, with no extraordinary events materially altering the company's audited financial picture.
