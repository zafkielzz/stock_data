### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2022, Vietjet Aviation Joint Stock Company (VJC) demonstrated a robust operational recovery, predominantly spearheaded by the domestic market. Specifically, Vietjet transported 20.6 million passengers across 116 thousand flights, with domestic passenger volume surging by 20% compared to pre-pandemic levels in 2019. Notably, 4Q22 domestic passenger traffic expanded more than 7-fold year-on-year, surpassing pre-Covid benchmarks. Core growth was catalyzed by primary business segments—including domestic and international passenger and cargo transportation—alongside ancillary revenue streams (seat selection, pre-ordered meals, baggage, duty-free retail, travel insurance, and shuttle services). On the international front, Vietjet aggressively expanded its footprint in India by launching over 20 new routes connecting major economic and tourism hubs such as New Delhi, Mumbai, Hanoi, and Ho Chi Minh City, bringing its total operating network to 103 routes as of December 31, 2022. Furthermore, direct support services (ground handling, technical maintenance, refueling) and aviation training (pilots, flight attendants, technicians) continued to solidify a closed-loop value chain, optimizing overall operating efficiency.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
Vietjet’s capital structure and liquidity faced significant pressure from working capital credit facilities and aircraft fleet investments. Ho Chi Minh City Development Joint Stock Commercial Bank (HDBank) acted as the largest strategic partner, extending credit lines of up to VND 4.3 trillion (pursuant to Resolution No. 48-22/VJC-HĐQT-NQ), with interest expenses paid by Vietjet totaling VND 124 billion for the year. Additionally, Vietjet maintained USD-denominated loans and financial support arrangements with related parties, including a USD 21.5 million loan extended to AAA Aircraft Asset Company Limited (maturing in 2025) and a USD 12.95+ million loan extended to Apricot Aircraft Assets No. 1 Limited (maturing in 2033) to support aircraft acquisitions and associated expenditures. Cash flow pressures were evidenced by the maintenance of high credit limits from commercial banks, long-term aircraft purchase/lease deposits with major OEMs such as Airbus S.A.S and Boeing Company, and long-term prepaid expenses for aircraft heavy maintenance and major overhauls.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Regarding credit risk and receivables, the report noted several short-term trade receivables from international clients (such as Hangzhou Star International Travel Service, Zhejiang Lixi International Travel, Hangzhou BaoLi, Yuejie Freight Forwarding, and Fadle Chem) overdue by more than three years. However, Management assesses these as fully recoverable upon the full recovery of the aviation market, supported by historical payment patterns and strategic cooperative ties. Regarding asset transactions and disposals, Vietjet executed an investment and equity transfer agreement for Pacific Star Investment and Development Company to a third party with a total transaction value of VND 2.22 trillion (structured over a 30-month payment period, of which VND 777 billion has been collected). Concerning post-balance sheet events and material related-party transactions, Vietjet repatriated retained earnings of USD 36.5 million from its subsidiary, Vietjet Air IVB No. 1 Limited, to the parent company to support operations, while also approving spare engine purchase and lease transactions, bringing total revenue from aircraft and engine trading activities to VND 3.807 trillion for the year.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
- **Environmental (E):** Jet fuel expenses accounted for a substantial share, ranging from 38% to 45%, of Vietjet’s total operating expenditures. Consequently, environmental management tools focused heavily on energy conservation and emission reduction. In FY2022, Vietjet saved over USD 5.4 million in fuel costs by optimizing cabin air-conditioning temperatures, calibrating taxi fuel, and reducing fuel burn inefficiencies, combined with an increased utilization of Ground Power Units (GPUs). The operational strategy of deploying a young, modern fleet equipped with New Engine Option (NEO) technology delivered a 20-30% reduction in fuel consumption and greenhouse gas emissions, alongside capacity optimization via high-density aircraft seating configurations. The information security management system is operated in compliance with ISO 27001:2013 standards and audited by TÜV NORD (Germany).
- **Social (S):** Vietjet prioritizes the development of high-quality human capital through comprehensive training programs at the Vietjet Aviation Academy (VJAA)—a large-scale training facility and Airbus’s sole approved type-rating training center in Vietnam, housing the country's largest fleet of A320 full flight simulators (FFSs). Human resources are rigorously managed across qualification tiers (postgraduate, undergraduate, college, vocational, and technical personnel), ensuring full adherence to labor regulations and aviation safety protocols.
- **Governance (G):** Vietjet maintains high standards of corporate transparency through strict compliance with bilingual information disclosure regulations (Vietnamese and English), safeguarding equitable access for both domestic and foreign investors. The company has established a dedicated Investor Relations (IR) division under the Finance and Accounting Department, responsible for regular earnings reporting, investor briefings, and full disclosure of remuneration, salaries, and bonuses for the Board of Directors, Supervisory Board, and Executive Management within General Meeting of Shareholders (AGM) documentation and Annual Reports.
