### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING

In FY2025, Hai An Transport and Stevedoring Joint Stock Company (HOSE: HAH) demonstrated exceptional equity market performance, posting a total return of +56.20%. The stock opened the period at VND 36,100/share and closed at VND 56,390/share, trading within a broad 52-week range of VND 32,860 to VND 66,090/share. Liquidity remained robust, with average matched volume reaching 2,533,855 shares per session. This upward momentum was underpinned by solid underlying fundamentals, highlighted by a trailing EPS of VND 6,987.92 and a resilient gross profit margin of 32.92%.

The company’s top-line growth was primarily driven by the operational recovery of its maritime shipping and port-handling segments across strategic logistical hubs spanning from North to South (Hanoi, Hai Phong, Da Nang, Ho Chi Minh City, and Ba Ria - Vung Tau). Operationally, executive management implemented a fleet capacity optimization strategy, proactively securing long-term strategic charterers for newly delivered 3,000 TEU and 7,000 TEU newbuilds, while simultaneously renegotiating expiring charter contracts ahead of 2026 at optimized rates and tenors. Although granular segment-level revenue and operating profit breakdowns were omitted from the reporting, HAH safeguarded its consolidated profitability through strict input cost controls and effective hedging against raw material volatility amidst broader inflationary pressures.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES

HAH maintains a prudent financial structure with a Debt-to-Equity (D/E) ratio of 51.02%. This leverage level is relatively conservative for a capital-intensive shipping enterprise, effectively optimizing shareholder capital returns within an operational framework yielding a Return on Equity (ROE) of 5.51% and a Return on Assets (ROA) of 3.08%. Cash flow pressures are primarily concentrated around debt service obligations for commercial bank loans utilized to fund the company’s fleet expansion strategy—specifically investments in container vessel newbuilds and port operational equipment.

Throughout 2025, benchmark interest rate fluctuations exerted a direct impact on HAH’s financing costs and capital expenditure cash flows. Despite directives from the State Bank of Vietnam urging credit institutions to curtail operational expenses and lower lending rates, HAH remained exposed to cost-of-capital risks given its substantial debt load. Notably, foreign exchange (FX) risk emerged as a tangible headwind, with the VND depreciating approximately 3.4% against the USD in the interbank market. While HAH’s domestic-centric procurement insulates its direct operating costs from currency shocks, its USD-denominated fixed-asset financing (vessel acquisitions) amplified principal and interest repayment burdens, alongside generating realized and unrealized foreign exchange losses. Detailed data regarding primary credit institutions and the exact maturity profile of short-term versus long-term debt was unavailable due to the exclusion of the financial statement notes.
