### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In fiscal year 2025, Vietnam National Petroleum Group (PLX) delivered resilient operational performance amidst heightened volatility in both global and domestic energy markets. Consolidated petroleum sales volume reached 17.7 million $m^3$/metric tons, representing a 12% year-over-year increase and aligning with management's targets, with retail volume expanding 7% YoY to 7.92 million $m^3$/metric tons. The core growth driver stems from the Group's entrenched market leadership, maintaining an estimated 40–42% domestic market share, which secures supply stability and optimizes its nationwide distribution network. Key competitive advantages include proprietary blending capabilities—specifically mastering the SAP blending technology for aviation fuel compliant with IATA and ICAO standards—the distribution of high-grade Euro V products, strategic international partnerships (Idemitsu, JX Nippon, Kuwait Petroleum, etc.), and structural tailwinds from the 2026–2030 public investment pipeline, providing a robust demand foundation for asphalt and fuel products. International operations, encompassing re-export, transshipment activities, and contributions from wholly-owned subsidiaries Petrolimex Singapore Pte. Ltd. and Petrolimex Laos Co., Ltd., further diversified revenue streams. Financially, consolidated revenue reached VND 309,875 billion, outperforming the full-year target by 25%, while consolidated profit before tax (PBT) came in at VND 3,643 billion (exceeding targets by 14%). Trailing twelve-month (TTM) earnings per share (EPS) stood at VND 1,530.20, with the stock trading at a P/E multiple of 25.85x and a P/B of 1.70x. The gross profit margin was 5.47%, while Return on Equity (ROE) and Return on Asset (ROA) printed at 0.45% and 0.17%, respectively.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
PLX's financial architecture in 2025 recorded a Debt-to-Equity (D/E) ratio of 69.51%, reflecting a prudently managed financial leverage profile aimed at supporting working capital requirements and infrastructure capital expenditures. The Group’s short-term borrowings comprise sub-12-month VND and USD-denominated credit facilities extended by commercial banks to the Parent Company, Petrolimex Aviation Fuel JSC, Petrolimex Petrochemical Corporation - JSC, Petrolimex Gas Corporation - JSC, Kien Giang Petroleum Co., Ltd., and various other subsidiaries. These short-term facilities are primarily deployed to fund working capital and open import letters of credit (LCs) for petroleum procurement, with interest rates floating in accordance with specific bank schedules and loan agreements. Meanwhile, long-term borrowings with tenors ranging from 13 to 120 months are maintained by subsidiaries (such as Petrolimex Specialized Chemical Transport JSC, VITACO, Petrolimex Waterway Petroleum Transport JSC, and Petrolimex Hai Phong Transport and Services JSC, among others) to finance the construction of retail service stations, acquisition of vessels and tank trucks, and other strategic projects in both VND and USD. All short- and long-term credit facilities are secured by a pool of the Group's tangible and intangible fixed assets, necessitating stringent operational cash flow management to service principal and interest obligations against a backdrop of volatile market interest rates.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
PLX’s risk management and provisioning framework for 2025 was closely tied to global petroleum price fluctuations, particularly the downward price trajectory observed in the final months of the year. The Group maintains valuation allowances for long-term financial investments and provisions for doubtful accounts in accordance with realized accounting book variances. Regarding material trade receivables, financial statement disclosures highlight transactions with major counterparties such as Vitol Asia Pte. Ltd., Vietjet Aviation JSC, and related parties (including Petrolimex Mechanical Engineering JSC, Construction Installation III - Petrolimex, and PJICO). These trade receivables are unsecured, non-interest bearing, and carry standard credit terms of 30 to 60 days. Notably, concerning the equity investment in Petrolimex Insurance Corporation (PJICO), the Executive Board evaluated the implications of State capital investment regulations under Decree 91/2015/NĐ-CP and Decree 32/2018/NĐ-CP, formulating a divestment plan (though the divestment had not materialized as of the reporting date of the consolidated financial statements). Additionally, the Group recognized specialized receivables, including overpaid environmental protection taxes levied by a subsidiary on export-destined goods, alongside receivables from the Ministry of Industry and Trade pertaining to storage fees for national strategic petroleum reserves (P10 inventory).

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
Throughout 2025, PLX aggressively advanced the integration of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) standards across its corporate ecosystem:
- **Environmental (E):** The Group focused on executing environmental initiatives centered on greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reduction across the supply chain, deploying advanced exhaust gas treatment systems and vapor recovery units at terminals and service stations. Emphasis was placed on enhancing oil spill response capabilities alongside a strategic pivot toward green energy infrastructure (modern port terminals, EV-integrated fueling stations, and rooftop solar installations). PLX is also actively evaluating participation in the carbon credit market and developing utility-scale renewable energy projects to advance its Net-Zero roadmap.
- **Social (S):** Occupational Health, Safety, and Environment (OHSE) and fire prevention standards were prioritized across all retail station and terminal operations. The Group sustained a competitive remuneration framework, with average employee compensation reaching VND 20.9 million per capita/month, complemented by specialized training programs in modern management, digital transformation, and risk governance. Furthermore, Petrolimex fulfilled its corporate social responsibility by allocating nearly VND 240 billion to philanthropic initiatives, highlighted by the "One Million National Flags to Support Fishermen at Sea" campaign.
- **Governance (G):** Corporate governance was bolstered by rationalizing and streamlining operations—consolidating 51 petroleum member companies down to 34 Single-Member Limited Liability Petroleum Companies and dissolving 3 corporate departments pursuant to Resolution 18-NQ/TW, thereby enhancing operational efficiency and managerial transparency. The Board of Directors maintained a balanced composition incorporating independent directors and foreign capital representatives (such as from strategic partner ENEOS), strictly adhering to securities regulations, best-practice corporate governance codes for public companies, and an integrated enterprise risk management framework to foster a culture of compliance and zero-tolerance toward corruption and waste.
