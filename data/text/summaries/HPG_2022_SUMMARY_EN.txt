### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING

FY2022 marked a period of profound headwinds for the broader financial markets and Hoa Phat Group's (HPG) equity valuation. HPG’s share price suffered a severe drawdown of 49.16%, opening at VND 23,920 and closing at VND 12,160, experiencing high volatility with an intraday/intra-period high of VND 26,830 and a low of VND 7,970. Nonetheless, market liquidity remained robust, with average trading volume exceeding 25.78 million shares per session. This steep repricing directly reflects cyclical pressures within the domestic steel sector alongside broader post-COVID macroeconomic softening.

Regarding operational performance, steel manufacturing and trading remained the core revenue driver, despite facing severe headwinds from the protracted stagnation of the domestic real estate market starting in H2 2022. The downturn in construction activity drove national steel consumption down by 7% YoY (to 27 million metric tons), domestic production down by 12% (to 29 million metric tons), and exports down sharply by nearly 20%. Amid this challenging operating environment, Hoa Phat's aggregate steel supply reached 7.2 million metric tons, representing a 7% contraction YoY. Segment operating margins experienced severe compression as raw material costs accounted for 70–75% of the cost of goods sold (COGS). Notably, the Russia-Ukraine conflict propelled coking coal prices up by 5x to 6x historical averages, compounded by persistent volatility in iron ore spot prices. Despite protectionist barriers and cheap Chinese steel imports, a key operational bright spot was Hoa Phat's effective utilization of free trade agreements (FTAs)—namely EVFTA, CPTPP, and RCEP—to scale up construction steel export volumes to nascent international markets. Beyond steel, the agricultural and real estate segments played ancillary roles but remained insufficient to offset the contraction in the core business.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES

Against a backdrop of monetary tightening and rising inflationary pressures, Hoa Phat proactively restructured its debt portfolio to mitigate interest rate and foreign exchange risks. Given the Group's high reliance on imported raw materials (coking coal and iron ore), the conglomerate was exposed to dual headwinds from sharp USD/VND fluctuations, which directly inflated working capital requirements and financing costs. To navigate these pressures, management optimized inventory holdings during periods of FX volatility and scaled up the deployment of currency hedging instruments with credit institutions.

Regarding liquidity management, a notable balance sheet development was the sharp decline in term deposits (ranging from 3 months to under 12 months) pledged as collateral with banks, which dropped precipitously from VND 7,935 billion as of January 1, 2022, to just VND 5.7 billion as of December 31, 2022. This substantial reduction indicates that the Group unlocked a significant pool of encumbered cash to fortify liquidity and support working capital needs during a trough in operating cash flow generation. Unpledged standard bank deposits yielded a widened interest rate range of 2.95% to 11.60% per annum, scaling up significantly from a ceiling of 7.80% per annum in the prior fiscal year, mirroring the broader macroeconomic monetary tightening cycle.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS

Mitigating downside valuation risks in iron ore and coking coal, Hoa Phat enforced a stringent inventory management policy and accelerated inventory turnover to minimize exposure to large-scale write-downs and inventory devaluation provisions. Within trade and other receivables as well as prepayments, the financial statements reflect a substantial allocation of capital deployed as advance payments to major international equipment and technology vendors—such as Primetals Technologies Japan and WISDRI—earmarked for upstream capacity expansion projects.

Other receivables captured site clearance compensation outlays in Hung Yen province to facilitate urban township and industrial park development projects. From a legal standpoint, the Group maintained rigorous oversight of international trade defense frameworks to preempt potential anti-dumping litigations in key export destinations. The FY2022 financial statements were independently audited by KPMG Limited, and executive management confirmed the absence of any material adjusting or non-adjusting events arising subsequent to December 31, 2022, requiring further disclosure.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION

**Environmental (E):** Hoa Phat focused on technical enhancements, the optimization of upstream production technologies, and the scaling of R&D initiatives to curtail energy intensity. The Group is progressively integrating circular economy frameworks across its logistics and manufacturing value chains to minimize the environmental externalities of heavy industrial waste.

**Social (S):** Corporate social responsibility (CSR) was steadfastly maintained under the guiding ethos of "harmonized growth with mutual development," backed by a total philanthropic budget of approximately VND 15 billion in FY2022. Key impact initiatives comprised: the "Heartbeat of Love" program, funding cardiac surgeries for 82 pediatric cases (60 successful interventions); the "Godmother" initiative, providing long-term sponsorship for 125 orphans across Hai Duong and Quang Ngai provinces; the "Spring of Love" campaign, distributing 6,000 Tet gift packages valued at VND 3 billion; free medical screening campaigns for underprivileged children in remote regions of Yen Bai; and recurring scholarship endowments extended to students at National Economics University (VND 935 million) and Hanoi University of Science and Technology (VND 100 million).

**Governance (G):** Hoa Phat’s corporate governance framework preserved structural stability under the leadership of Chairman Tran Dinh Long and CEO Nguyen Viet Thang. The Group demonstrated high regulatory compliance with pivotal legislative updates, including the 2020 Enterprise Law, the 2019 Securities Law, and the 2020 Investment Law. Financial reporting systems remained subject to independent audits by KPMG, ensuring robust information transparency for equity holders and the broader market.
