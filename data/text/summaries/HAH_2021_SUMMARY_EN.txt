1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING

Hai An Transport and Stevedoring JSC (HAH) recorded an explosive trading year in FY2021, delivering an impressive total return of +289.62% (opening price: VND 5,200; closing price: VND 20,260; trading range: VND 4,160 – VND 23,490), underpinned by robust liquidity with an average matched trading volume of 1,579,480 shares per session. This equity price performance directly reflects the company's exceptional earnings growth amid severe global supply chain disruptions induced by the COVID-19 pandemic. Severe congestion at major container ports in the US, Europe, and China drove average container freight rates up by 5x to 6x compared to pre-pandemic levels. 

HAH aggressively capitalized on this cyclical tailwind by proactively expanding its vessel fleet. Commencing in Q4 2021, the company deployed two newly acquired container vessels (1,740 – 1,800 TEU capacity) on long-term time charters in the international market at lucrative freight rates, propelling Q4 and full-year 2021 net profit to triple management’s initial targets. Domestically, despite industrial lockdowns in Southern provinces during Q3 (which contributed to a 6.2% contraction in GDP), the economy rebounded strongly in Q4 with GDP growth printing at 5.2%. The structural shift of export flows toward Northern Vietnam—combined with a record nationwide trade turnover of USD 688.5 billion (+22.6% YoY), comprising exports of USD 336.25 billion (+19.0% YoY) and imports of USD 332.25 billion (+26.5% YoY)—generated a solid domestic demand foundation, supporting high growth momentum across HAH’s port operations and logistics services.

2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES

Due to the absence of the detailed Notes to the Financial Statements in the source documentation, granular figures regarding short- and long-term debt balances, floating versus fixed interest rate structures, and the identity of HAH’s primary credit-providing financial institutions were not disclosed. 

Nonetheless, management’s report highlights a key structural risk stemming from the utilization of foreign currency-denominated debt (USD) to finance capital expenditures for fixed asset acquisitions (predominantly the container vessel fleet). Maintaining USD-denominated liabilities exposes HAH directly to foreign exchange risk arising from USD/VND volatility. In 2021, this pressure was partially mitigated by the appreciation of the VND, supported by subdued domestic USD demand in Q3, robust foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows, and Vietnam’s persistently high foreign exchange reserves. Conversely, the company bears no foreign exchange risk regarding input material costs, as its raw material supplies are sourced entirely domestically.

3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS

Specific figures regarding inventory write-downs, allowances for doubtful accounts, off-balance-sheet guarantees, or ongoing legal disputes are omitted from the current documentation due to the lack of the Financial Statement Notes. 

From a qualitative perspective, HAH’s primary operational risk lies in bunker fuel price volatility, which is entirely exogenous and dictated by global commodity markets, forcing the company to continuously adjust service pricing to preserve profit margins. Furthermore, the refrigerated container (reefer) segment is highly vulnerable to shifting cross-border trade policies and stringent quarantine controls enforced by China. Domestically, HAH faces intense price competition in the Hai Phong region, where industry peers have demonstrated a willingness to slash tariffs by up to 20% or even operate at a loss to capture market share. Long-term competitive pressures are also mounting with the operational rollout of the Lach Huyen deep-water port, which exerts pricing pressure on traditional river ports such as Hai An Port. To hedge against these risks, HAH has proactively established its own domestic container shipping route and forged alliances with foreign shipping lines to maintain short-haul feeder services, thereby securing stable throughput volumes for its port terminals.

4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION

HAH did not issue a standalone Sustainability Report for FY2021; consequently, quantitative environmental (E) metrics—such as Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions (Scopes 1, 2, and 3) or specific energy-efficiency initiatives—were not formally detailed. 

Regarding the social (S) dimension, the company focused on strengthening its human capital through targeted recruitment of young, high-caliber professionals via direct channels and executive search firms (headhunters) to enhance managerial efficiency and operational execution across its integrated service chain. 

On the governance (G) front, HAH maintains a standard corporate governance structure comprising the General Meeting of Shareholders (GMS), the Board of Directors (BoD), the Supervisory Board, and the Chief Executive Officer (CEO). The BoD consists of seven members—notably featuring Ms. Tran Thi Hai Yen as an Independent Board Member—ensuring objective oversight of executive management. The Supervisory Board comprises three members who are entirely independent of the BoD and executive management, fulfilling a mandate to oversee accounting and financial integrity to safeguard the ultimate interests of shareholders.
