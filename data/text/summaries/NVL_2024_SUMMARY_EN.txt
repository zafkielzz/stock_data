1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2024, No Va Land Investment Group Corporation (NVL) faced severe headwinds stemming from persistent macroeconomic pressures and unresolved legal bottlenecks across its flagship projects. The Group's core operational drivers remained concentrated in real estate development—encompassing residential properties, large-scale coastal tourism, and entertainment urban complexes such as NovaWorld Phan Thiet and NovaWorld Ho Tram—alongside construction, management consulting, and brokerage services. Despite executive management's concerted efforts to restart project execution, accelerate construction, and drive unit handovers to restore market confidence, the Group posted a net loss after tax of VND 4,395 billion for the year. On the equity market, NVL shares experienced a volatile and negative performance, closing the year at VND 9,550, representing a 40.42% contraction from its opening price of VND 16,030, against an average matched trading volume of 16,730,525 shares per session.

2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
Capital structure and acute liquidity pressure remained critical challenges for Novaland throughout FY2024. As of December 31, 2024, the Group's aggregate principal debt—comprising bank borrowings and bond issuances—expanded to VND 61,566 billion, compared to VND 57,712 billion at year-end 2023. Nevertheless, the Group achieved notable headway in debt restructuring, substantially completing the resolution of a major portion of its loan and bond portfolio, including the servicing of VND 7,057 billion in principal. To secure funding for key developments, Novaland secured supplementary credit commitments totaling VND 18,200 billion from domestic tier-1 banking partners, including MB Bank, VP Bank, TP Bank, and MSB. Furthermore, the Group reached preliminary debt restructuring agreements with creditors and bondholders amounting to VND 17,898 billion, successfully negotiated bond extensions under Decree 08/2023/NĐ-CP at an applicable coupon rate of 11.5% p.a., and finalized restructuring terms for USD 300 million of international convertible bonds listed on the Singapore Exchange.

3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Novaland’s FY2024 consolidated financial statements recorded negative operating cash flows of VND 5,971 billion (compared to a negative VND 7,626 billion in FY2023). As of the reporting date, the enterprise had yet to fulfill certain short-term debt service obligations regarding maturing borrowings and bonds. Crucially, the Group breached payment deadlines on several credit facilities and international financial institution contracts totaling VND 5,922 billion, although these lenders have not yet initiated formal legal action. These factors, compounded by the recurring net loss, exert material downward pressure and cast substantial doubt on the entity’s going concern assumption. To alleviate liquidity strain, executive management has formulated an asset divestment plan targeting an aggregate expected value of VND 26,542 billion across 21 assets. To date, the Group has successfully monetized 2 assets yielding VND 8,059 billion, executed principle agreements for 7 assets (VND 7,433 billion), and secured memoranda of understanding for 5 assets (VND 6,682 billion).

4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
Aligning with its long-term growth strategy, Novaland is actively executing a robust transition toward Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) standards to strengthen risk-mitigation capabilities and attract international green capital flows.
- **Environmental (E):** The Group is committed to adhering to globally recognized green building and operational benchmarks, including GRESB, EDGE, and LEED. It prioritizes the development of eco-friendly, smart urban centers that integrate energy-efficiency solutions and environmental impact management across the entire project ecosystem.
- **Social (S):** Novaland places strong emphasis on human capital development, maintaining a workforce of 1,093 employees as of year-end 2024. The Group upholds progressive human resource policies, professional development and training programs, and robust welfare benefits, thereby fostering a professional, safe, and engaging workplace that aligns corporate values with community and customer prosperity.
- **Governance (G):** Corporate governance has been systematically streamlined to ensure operational leanness, clear decentralization, and transparency. The Board of Directors maintains an independent and non-executive representation exceeding one-third to strengthen objective oversight. The 2024 Annual Report and Sustainability Report are presented as an integrated report, complying with statutory disclosure regulations (Circular 96/2020/TT-BTC), referencing international frameworks set by the IIRC and OECD, and independently audited by Moore AISC Auditing and Informatics Services Co., Ltd.
