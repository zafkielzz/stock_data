Below is the institutional-grade equity research summary translation for Hoang Anh Gia Lai JSC (HAG) for FY2021:

### 1. BUSINESS DRIVERS & SEGMENT REPORTING
In FY2021, HAG reported consolidated net revenue of VND 2,097bn, representing a 34% decline compared to VND 3,177bn in FY2020, primarily driven by extensive corporate restructuring and the divestment of subsidiaries under Hoang Anh Gia Lai Agricultural JSC (HNG). Despite the top-line contraction, gross profit registered a favorable growth of 507 billion VND, underpinned by a strategic pivot toward two core pillars: fruit cultivation (specifically bananas) and pig farming. Specifically, the fruit segment was the largest contributor, generating VND 1,004bn in revenue (47.9% of total) with an impressive gross margin of 41.7%. The pig farming segment initially contributed VND 558bn in revenue (26.6% of total) with a gross margin of 34.4%. Key export markets remained China, Japan, South Korea, and Singapore, with China offering significant potential albeit facing headwinds from stringent pandemic control policies.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
HAG’s capital and debt structure demonstrated notable positive improvements in FY2021 following the divestment of HNG-group subsidiaries to alleviate bank debt pressure. Total liabilities dropped sharply by 49.5% to VND 13,767bn, comprising VND 6,755bn in short-term debt and VND 7,012bn in long-term debt. The debt-to-total-assets ratio was maintained at 0.45x, while the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 1.77x. However, cash flow and capital structure pressures persist as short-term borrowings include unsecured and secured loans from major commercial banks (such as BIDV, Sacombank, and TPBank) carrying floating interest rates ranging from 7.5% to over 10% p.a. Balancing principal and interest repayment cash flows continues to require tight monitoring of collection progress from new agricultural projects by the Group.

### 3. PROVISIONING RISKS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & SUBSEQUENT EVENTS
HAG adopted a prudent provisioning approach for doubtful receivables and inventories to accurately reflect financial risks. Short-term doubtful debt provisions reached VND 1,467bn against total overdue receivables, reflecting pressures associated with legacy partners and off-balance-sheet customers. Guarantee commitments and contingent liabilities related to third-party borrowings continued to be closely monitored through credit agreements and collateral pledges (land use rights, rubber plantations, and banana orchards). Regarding post-balance-sheet events (up to early 2022), the Group completed further HNG share transfer transactions, reducing its ownership stake to 11.73% in February 2022 and 9.4% in March 2022, thereby further de-leveraging its balance sheet and channeling resources entirely into the new agricultural model.

### 4. ESG EXECUTION
- **Environmental (E):** HAG focuses on deploying advanced agricultural solutions to minimize negative environmental impacts, notably implementing the Israeli drip irrigation system which saves 50–70% of irrigation water while strictly limiting fertilizer and pesticide residues from leaching into the soil. The enterprise also emphasizes the reuse of packaging materials and PVC/HDPE pipelines, alongside installing solar energy systems and researching livestock waste circularity solutions to produce organic fertilizer for crops.
- **Social (S):** The Group maintains a human resources policy prioritizing local labor, particularly ethnic minority communities in Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia, ensuring female employees account for 40% of the workforce and female managers comprise 35%. HAG fully complies with social and health insurance regulations, organizes regular health check-ups for employees via the Hoang Anh Gia Lai - University of Medicine and Pharmacy Hospital network, and constructs residential quarters and daycares to support the material and spiritual well-being of workers across its agricultural farms.
- **Governance (G):** Corporate governance strictly adheres to the Law on Enterprises and the Company’s Charter. The Board of Directors maintains transparency with the participation of independent members, coordinating closely with the Supervisory Board and reputable independent auditing firms (such as Ernst & Young Vietnam) to review periodic financial statements, ensure compliance with disclosure standards, and target sustainable development aligned with international benchmarks.
