1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING

The maritime transportation segment continued to serve as the core growth engine for Hai An Transport and Stevedoring Joint Stock Company (HAH) in FY2022, driven by an aggressive fleet expansion strategy. The company successfully invested in and took delivery of the container vessel HAIAN CITY (capacity: 1,577 TEUs) in March and the HAIAN ROSE (capacity: 1,708 TEUs) in November, bringing the total fleet size to 11 vessels with an aggregate carrying capacity of nearly 16,000 TEUs. Additional top-line momentum was generated by the launch of an intra-Asia container route (Hai Phong – Hong Kong – Nansha – Hai Phong) serviced by the HAIAN BELL (1,200 TEUs), marking HAH’s maiden direct penetration into the Mainland Chinese market.

From a macroeconomic perspective, Vietnam’s full-year import-export turnover posted positive growth, with exports reaching $371.9 billion (up 10.6% YoY) and imports reaching $360.7 billion (up 8.4% YoY). However, sector momentum exhibited clear divergence, decelerating progressively from Q2 through Q4. This slowdown was primarily catalyzed by the U.S. Federal Reserve’s aggressive monetary tightening cycle, which dampened global consumer demand, compounded by China’s "Zero-COVID" policy that caused severe cross-border supply chain bottlenecks. Consequently, transportation demand and port throughput volumes contracted directly, most notably in the refrigerated container (reefer) segment.

2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES

Owing to the domestic sourcing of its operational inputs, HAH is insulated from direct foreign exchange volatility on its operational expenditures. Nevertheless, capital structure pressures are heavily concentrated in foreign currency-denominated (USD) debt facilities utilized to finance major capital expenditures on fixed assets, specifically container vessel acquisition and newbuilding projects (namely the HAIAN CITY and HAIAN ROSE). 

While the USD/VND exchange rate in Vietnam demonstrated superior stability relative to regional peers, the sharp appreciation of the greenback in global currency markets generated latent FX translation and transaction risks on foreign currency debt obligations. Furthermore, rising floating borrowing rates across global financial markets represent an extant headwind, presenting a material risk of escalating financial expenses and adversely impacting operating cash flows during upcoming debt service and principal repayment cycles.

3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS

Available documentation does not disclose granular disclosures regarding inventory obsolescence provisions or doubtful debt allowances. Nonetheless, HAH’s most salient operational risk stems from intensifying competitive pressures within the Hai Phong port cluster. The commissioning of the Lach Huyen International Gateway Port (operational since 2018)—capable of accommodating vessels up to 100,000 DWT—has exerted direct competitive pressure on terminals situated in the Dinh Vu and Song Cam sectors, including the Hai An Port. Price-cutting competition has turned fierce, with numerous industry peers willing to slash service tariffs by up to 20% or operate at a net loss to capture market share.

A critical operational post-balance sheet event materialized immediately following December 31, 2022, with the International Maritime Organization (IMO) formally enforcing Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index (EEXI) and operational carbon intensity rating mechanisms, alongside the strict implementation of Energy Efficiency Existing Ship operational speed optimization (EPL - Engine Power Limitation) measures effective January 1, 2023, to curb carbon emissions. Concurrently, regulations enforcing the use of marine gas oil (MGO) with a sulfur content below 0.1% within Emission Control Areas (ECAs) were tightened. These regulatory shifts are projected to significantly inflate bunker fuel expenses and constrain the operational efficiency of HAH’s fleet moving into FY2023.

4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION

*   **Environmental (E):** HAH faces rigorous compliance mandates regarding stringent global environmental standards enforced by the IMO. The company is legally mandated to execute technical remediations, such as the installation of Engine Power Limitation (EPL) devices to control vessel speeds and the transition to high-grade Marine Gas Oil (MGO with sulfur content <0.1%) when operating within Emission Control Areas (ECAs).
*   **Social (S):** HAH maintains and expands its logistics service network across strategic economic hubs (Hanoi, Hai Phong, Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang, Ba Ria - Vũng Tau), contributing to the resilience of the national supply chain while safeguarding stable employment for its workforce amidst a challenging maritime shipping environment.
*   **Governance (G):** HAH’s corporate governance framework is structured along standardized tiers comprising the General Meeting of Shareholders (GMS), the Board of Directors (comprising 7 members), the Supervisory Board (comprising 3 independent members distinct from the Executive Board), and the Board of Management. Board independence is underscored by the participation of 1 independent director, Ms. Tran Thi Hai Yen. During FY2022, the executive leadership underwent a notable personnel transition, with the company officially revoking the title of Deputy General Director from Mr. Tran Tien Dung, effective June 10, 2022. To date, the company has not published comprehensive reports detailing the adoption of specific GRI standards or SDGs.
