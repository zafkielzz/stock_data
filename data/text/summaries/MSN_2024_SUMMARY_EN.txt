### 1. BUSINESS DYNAMICS & SEGMENT REPORTING
FY2024 marked a pivotal structural transformation for Masan Group, highlighted by consolidated net profit after tax attributable to shareholders surging by 377.5% YoY to VND 1,999 billion. Core earnings growth was propelled by the Group's integrated consumer-retail ecosystem strategy. Specifically, Masan Consumer (MCH) reinforced its market leadership across condiments, convenience foods, and energy drinks, while successfully expanding into personal and home care. WinCommerce (WCM) cemented its position as Vietnam's largest modern grocery retailer, executing a successful turnaround from an annual net loss exceeding USD 200 million to a sustainable positive net profit, with MCH's sales within the WCM network growing by 84%. Masan MEATLife (MML) posted its maiden operating profit since the divestment of its feed segment, while average sales per outlet doubled, driven by ecosystem synergies. Phúc Long Heritage (PLH) maintained the highest EBITDA margin among domestic tea and coffee chains. Internationally, the Group executed a strategic restructuring by finalizing the divestment of its entire economic interest in H.C. Starck Holding (HCS) to the Mitsubishi Materials Corporation Group for total consideration of USD 134.5 million, thereby mitigating exposure to non-core high-tech materials.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
Masan Group’s capital structure in FY2024 centered around robust treasury management and credit operations involving short- and long-term borrowings, alongside financial deposit transactions. Regarding bank credit facilities, a portion of cash and cash equivalents (carrying value of VND 508,991 million as of December 31, 2024, down from VND 641,542 million at the beginning of the year), alongside certain short-term receivables, were pledged as collateral to secure borrowings for the Group and its subsidiaries. Regarding internal loans and advances, the Group recorded loans extended to key management personnel and a founding shareholder who also serves as a key manager of an indirect subsidiary; these loans were secured by 7,170,640 shares, bore a fixed interest rate of 12.0% p.a., and matured on October 31, 2024. Cash flow pressures and working capital management were balanced through short-term deposits (VND 18,611,000 million) and long-term deposits (VND 6,531,500 million) placed with corporate partners for investment purposes, through which the Group earned returns pursuant to respective contracts while upholding stringent financial discipline to optimize its cost of capital.

### 3. PROVISIONS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & POST-BALANCE SHEET EVENTS
Provisions and legal risk management in FY2024 were characterized by investment provisioning and disputes over state financial obligations. Notably, as part of the HCS divestment transaction, the long-term investment in Nyobolt Limited—a UK-based firm specializing in tungsten- and niobium-enabled fast-charging battery solutions—was transferred internally to The SHERPA Co., Ltd. for USD 19.8 million, with a corresponding investment provision against Nyobolt recognized on the consolidated financial statements. Regarding contingent risks and legal disputes, other short-term receivables included VND 980,846 million pertaining to supplemental mineral exploitation rights fees ("TCQKTKS") for the Nui Phao mine (via subsidiary NPM) for the 2015–2024 period. NPM contested the methodology applied in Official Dispatch No. 3724 and Decision No. 1640 issued by regulatory authorities, arguing that the regulators erroneously applied the resource tax valuation price of crude tungsten ore rather than finished industrial products as mandated by Decree No. 12/2015/NĐ-CP and Circular No. 152/2015/TT-BTC, thereby creating a contingent liability requiring close ongoing surveillance.

### 4. ESG IMPLEMENTATION
Masan Group continued to embed its "Doing well by doing good" ethos into its sustainable development strategy, anchored by a three-pillar framework aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and IFC Performance Standards (IFC PSs).
- **Environment (E):** The Group focused on four material pillars: greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions inventory, health and nutrition, supply chain management, water conservation, and the circular economy. Masan reaffirmed its commitment to reducing Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions by 15% by 2030, progressing toward a Net Zero target by 2050; concurrently, it targeted a 5% to 10% reduction in water consumption per unit of production and a 50% increase in recycled waste by 2025.
- **Social (S):** Masan prioritized human capital development, targeting an annual employee satisfaction rate of at least 70%. The Group maintained a female representation of 38% at managerial Grade 3 and above—outperforming the Asia-Pacific regional average—while sustaining a score of at least 70% in Diversity and Inclusion metrics. For consumers, customer satisfaction is targeted at 90% by 2030, supported by a loyalty ecosystem aiming for 30 million members.
- **Governance (G):** Sustainable governance was reinforced through the Group-wide ESG Committee (established and operationalized in 2023–2024), tasked with overseeing ESG strategies. The Group strictly enforces its Code of Conduct and Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption policies, ensuring 100% of employees undergo annual refresher training to uphold the highest standards of business ethics.
