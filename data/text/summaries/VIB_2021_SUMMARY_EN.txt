Below is the institutional-grade translation of the financial report summary for Vietnam International Commercial Joint Stock Bank (VIB):

***

### 1. BUSINESS DRIVERS & SEGMENT REPORTING
Fiscal year 2021 marked a strong breakthrough for VIB despite the impact of the fourth wave of the COVID-19 pandemic. Profit before tax reached VND 8,011 billion, representing a 38% increase compared to 2020. Core growth drivers stemmed from the strategic focus on retail banking, which currently accounts for 87% of the credit portfolio. This expansion was propelled by a 19.1% growth in outstanding credit balances and an optimized net interest margin (NIM) of 4.4%. The credit card and insurance (bancassurance) segments remained the primary non-interest income generators, maintaining market leadership for consecutive years. VIB successfully digitalized its products and services entirely, driving down the cost-to-income ratio (CIR) from 39.8% in 2020 to 35.5% in 2021. Although operating as a domestic commercial bank, close partnerships with international institutions such as the ADB and IFC enabled VIB to diversify its funding sources, strongly supporting revenue growth from financial services.

### 2. CAPITAL STRUCTURE, DEBT & INTEREST RATES
VIB’s capital structure in 2021 recorded robust improvements, with total mobilized funds reaching VND 280,320 billion, up 27% year-on-year. Notably, Current Account Savings Account (CASA) deposits posted an impressive 55% growth, playing a pivotal role in lowering input funding costs. Regarding equity structure, VIB completed a 40% stock dividend payout, increasing its charter capital to VND 15,531 billion. This raised the Basel II Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) to 11.69%, significantly higher than the 8% regulatory minimum. In terms of foreign currency funding, VIB effectively leveraged capital streams from international partners such as the ADB (approximately USD 260 million) and major global banks (USD 350 million), ensuring robust medium- and long-term liquidity. Cash flow pressure was tightly controlled, with the ratio of short-term funds used for medium- and long-term loans maintained at 21.94%, well below the State Bank of Vietnam’s (SBV) 37% cap.

### 3. PROVISIONING RISKS, CONTINGENT LIABILITIES & SUBSEQUENT EVENTS
VIB maintained a prudent risk management policy amid the pandemic. The Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio stood at 1.75%, remaining well-controlled below the 3% threshold. The bank proactively allocated VND 1,598 billion in credit risk provisions to cushion against fluctuations arising from debt restructuring under Circular 01/NHNN. For off-balance-sheet commitments, VIB stepped up risk controls through rigorous approval workflows, particularly for issuance guarantees and trade finance commitments. No material legal disputes arose that would affect the financial position after December 31, 2021. This stability was reinforced by the successful implementation of Basel II pillars and independent audits conducted by a reputable firm (KPMG), ensuring maximum transparency for stakeholders.

### 4. ESG EXECUTION
- **Environmental (E):** VIB prioritized digital transformation (going paperless) to reduce emissions from office operations. The bank promoted green credit standards in project appraisal while optimizing energy consumption across its headquarters system through the installation of energy-efficient equipment.
- **Social (S):** With 9,658 employees, VIB placed strong emphasis on gender equality, with females accounting for 56% of the workforce and 55.2% of management positions. The bank maintained competitive employee benefits and delivered 121,408 hours of training in 2021, ensuring the sustainable development of human capital during the pandemic.
- **Governance (G):** VIB’s governance system strictly adheres to the international three lines of defense model. The Board of Directors includes independent members, ensuring objectivity in decision-making. Information disclosure is conducted transparently on the HOSE, complying with the corporate governance regulations of the State Securities Commission (SSC) and international standards such as those of the IFC.
